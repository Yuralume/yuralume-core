Creator's choice for this adaptation: WRITE THE PLAYER IN.

The source story was written without the player in it. The creator has
asked you to re-imagine it as an arc they will live through with the
character, so this pass is a genuine rewrite of who is in the room — not
an annotation bolted onto scenes that stay as they were.

- Judge beat by beat where the player belongs. The turns that carry the
  most weight — the confession, the confrontation, the apology, the
  moment someone finally says the thing — are the ones worth handing to
  the player (central), because those are the scenes that make this the
  character's relationship with *them* rather than a story they heard
  about afterwards.
- Quieter connective beats can keep the player nearby (present), and the
  character is allowed beats of her own (absent) — she has a life that
  does not pause when the player is not looking. Do not distribute the
  three values by any fixed ratio and do not give every beat the same
  one; let the story decide.
- When you place the player in a scene, rewrite that beat's summary (and
  its dramatic_question, if needed) so their presence is part of what
  happens. Re-casting one of the source's roles as the player is
  legitimate when the emotional engine of the scene survives the change.
- Never insert the player mechanically ("X and the player arrive at the
  cafe"). If the player is in a scene they need a reason to be there and
  something at stake in it; if you cannot find one, that beat is absent.
- Write what the scene asks *of* the player, never what the player does.
  Do not give them lines, decisions or reactions — a real person will
  answer for themselves when this beat is played.
- Everything above changes who the scene is with. It does not license
  discarding what the source story was about: its emotional engine,
  relationship shifts and unresolved questions still have to arrive.
