# Update 2026.07.31

1. Same-scene mode no longer asks for permission: if you say you're there, you're there. Switching happens instantly, and the system will never again rule on whether your presence is "plausible" and block or warn you — where you are is yours to decide.
2. Characters now react to your arrival for real: she plays it from her own schedule — startled if you show up while she's out running errands, everyday warmth if she's home. In moments she'd rather be alone, she draws the line in character — asking you to wait, talking through the door — instead of the system refusing you on her behalf. And she'll never invent what you did or how you got there.
3. Characters always know what they're doing right now: whether you're sharing the scene or texting, she carries her current moment — what she's in the middle of, what time it is — into the conversation, so scene and time no longer drift apart.
4. Story chapters stop replaying the same tune: when planning a new chapter, the story looks back at earlier ones to avoid running the same plot in a new coat, and draws fresh material from the bundled story-seed pool; when things have been flat for too long, it deliberately brings in something new from outside the conversation to stir the still water.
